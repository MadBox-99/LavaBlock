Version: 0.2.0
Date: 2026-09-24
  Features:
    - Added the Air Filter. The air line's second building, and the one it has always been missing: the air compressor used to compress the air, filter it and then pull the metal back out of it, which is three jobs in one box. Filtering moves out to a machine of its own, so the compressor compresses and separates and the filter filters. It runs the cleaning step at the speed the compressor did, on well under half the power, because charging a plate is cheap next to squeezing steam
    - Chlorine can finally be obtained, and it finally does something. Like argon it has been in this world from the start as a fluid you could barrel and unbarrel and nothing else. A chemical plant now scrubs it out of the gas the lava gives off - a real volcano vents hydrogen chloride along with the steam, and this island is one enormous vent
    - The Lava Centrifuge takes a chlorinated purification recipe. The plain pass throws away half the melt, because the iron and sulphur it is shedding leave as part of the fraction it spins off; chlorine carries them away as chlorides instead and the melt they were suspended in stays. A thousand lava and fifty chlorine give 750 purified where a thousand alone give 500. Counted back to raw lava that is one and a half in for one out instead of two, a quarter less lava through the pumps for every drop of purified. It runs slower than the plain pass, so a plain centrifuge is still worth keeping where throughput matters more than plumbing
    - Added the Gas Combiner, a 3x3 blending skid: two receivers, a mixing drum with a lit sight glass, and a blower. It meters two gases together in a fixed ratio and pipes out the blend
    - Argon can finally be obtained. It has been in this world all along as a fluid you could barrel and unbarrel and nothing else - no recipe in the game produced a single unit of it. The air compressor now separates it out of air, at the poor yield a gas that is one per cent of the atmosphere deserves
    - The Crystallizer takes a third recipe, its best. Argon blended with a trace of oxygen makes a shielding gas, and a hearth grown under one yields seven crystals where clean melt alone gives five. Pure argon would be wrong here: silica is an oxide, and a melt blanketed in nothing but inert gas comes back reduced. The machine has a second inlet for it, a thin brass one on the opposite flank from the melt
    - Added the Water Condenser, a 3x3 machine that condenses steam back into water at two to one. There is no water anywhere on the lava island, so the only supply was the vanilla steam condensation recipe in a chemical plant, which hands back less than a tenth of the steam you feed it and made liquid nitrogen and smelting coolant far dearer than they were meant to be. Unlocked by a technology of its own once you have Calcite Processing and chemical science
    - Added the Arboretum, a 5x5 glasshouse that grows timber. Tree seeds and water go in, logs come out, and the same building also turns logs back into seeds, so one machine closes the whole cycle. Four planting beds sit under magenta grow lamps with an irrigation boom turning slowly overhead. Researching it hands over a batch of seeds, so the first crop can always be started
    - Added the Algae Tank, a sealed photobioreactor that grows algae. Three strains live off three different solutions, and each concentrates something worth pressing back out of the harvest: green algae feeds on mineral-rich water and presses into timber at about the same cost as the Arboretum, blue algae fixes nitrogen out of plain air and makes liquid nitrogen roughly a third cheaper than the lava route, and red algae grows on hot water and lays down calcite for well under half what synthesising it costs. Which strain you grow is decided by what you pipe into the second inlet, and the culture in the columns takes on that strain's colour. Green and red come with the technology; blue waits for a Nitrogen Fixation technology alongside the air compressor line, because until then there is no air to feed it
    - Added the Bio Garden, which presses the harvest. Growing and pressing are different jobs and each got its own building: the tank raises the culture where you can watch it, the garden squeezes it for what it grew on. All three extraction recipes run there
    - While it runs, the Bio Garden scrubs pollution out of the air around it, about as much as seventeen trees would - which matters on an island that has none, and where the only thing soaking up a factory's smoke is the lava itself. It only cleans while it is actually crafting, so an idle garden does nothing
    - Oxygen is now made by electrolysing water in a chemical plant, and it is worth making: oxygen-enriched iron and copper smelting produce the same molten metal from half the lava. Lava was never scarce, but 5000 of it per craft is what forces a wall of pumps behind every smelter, so this trades a gas line against half the plumbing. Unlocked by an Oxygen Processing technology of its own
    - Added the roll crusher, and with it a second source of stone. Until now every single stone came from ore clearing, which burns an iron ore and a copper ore apiece, so expanding the island cost around a hundred thousand lava in stone alone. Lava can now be quenched into basalt in a chemical plant and a crusher grinds it down: about a hundred and seventy lava a stone instead of four hundred, at the price of a second machine, water and a hungry motor. It takes two passes - the first breaks basalt into gravel, the second grinds the gravel to stone - so a rock line is a bank of crushers feeding another bank rather than one machine bolted to a belt. Unlocked early, by Basalt Casting and then Stone Crushing on red and green science, because land expansion is available from the first minute and should not wait for chemical science
    - The crusher comes in three. The Burner Roll Crusher burns fuel and needs no power line at all, so the first stone can be crushed before the grid reaches that corner of the island; the Roll Crusher runs on electricity at twice the speed and takes two modules; and the Industrial Roll Crusher is faster again with four module slots. Each is built out of the one below it and fast-replaces it, so upgrading never means tearing out the belts
    - The Industrial Roll Crusher takes a lubricant line, and with it a grinding recipe that gets half again as much stone out of the same gravel - six instead of four. That drops the cost of a stone to about a hundred and ten lava, a bit over a quarter of what ore clearing charges. There is deliberately no lubricated version of brick crushing: at that yield the brick round trip would break even and start printing stone out of nothing
    - The new machines are built out of parts of their own rather than a pile of plates. A crusher roll is a toothed drum, and two of them are what a roll crusher crushes with; a culture column is what algae is grown in, and the tank stands four; a grow lamp is the magenta tube the arboretum's beds and the tank's columns sit under; a condenser coil is the finned copper tube that pulls the heat out of steam. Each is something you can point at on the finished machine, each is hand-craftable, and each arrives with the technology of the first machine that wants it
    - Surplus stone brick can still be ground back into stone, but it now has a technology of its own, Brick Reclamation, that follows Stone Crushing. It is a side door you only want once cryogenic lava cooling is running and the brick is piling up, and it no longer arrives with the crusher itself
    - Added the Crystallizer, a 3x3 hexagonal hearth that grows crystal out of molten rock. Lava is held just under the temperature it freezes at and stirred slowly, and silica crystal grows on the shelf around the pan. Remelt the crystal fast in an ordinary furnace and it cannot crystallise again - that is glass, and it is the first transparent material the island has ever had
    - Glass goes into a glazed panel, and the glazed panel is what the glasshouses are actually built from. The Arboretum, the Algae Tank and the Bio Garden's dome are all described as glass and were all made of steel plate; now they are made of what they look like. Culture columns take raw glass rather than a flat panel, because a column is a tube
    - The Crystallizer takes a better recipe once the Lava Centrifuge is running. Clean melt seeds far fewer grains, so purified lava grows five large crystals where raw lava gives two small ones - a little over three times the yield counted all the way back to raw lava. Unlocked by Refined Crystallization
  Balancing:
    - Shielded crystallizing is an addition, not a replacement. Forty per cent more crystal costs a whole gas line - argon off the air compressor, a combiner to blend it, and a pipe run to the hearth's second inlet - so a base running well on purified lava is under no obligation to build any of it
    - The rock line is longer but no dearer. Two basalt still make four stone end to end; what changed is that it takes two passes to get there instead of one, so the cost of the extra step is a second crusher and the belt between them rather than a worse ratio
    - The glasshouses cost the same steel they always did. Each building's plate count drops by roughly the steel its new glazed panels carry, so glass lengthens the road to a glasshouse rather than putting a toll on it
    - Lava Crystallization sits in front of the Arboretum, which is a new step on an existing path. It is deliberately one of the cheapest researches in the mod - 75 on red and green science, no new science pack - for exactly that reason
    - Researches that used to hand over several machines at once have been split up. Basalt Casting, Stone Crushing and Brick Reclamation were one technology that dropped a machine and three recipes in one go; Algae Cultivation and the Bio Garden were one that dropped two buildings and six recipes. Each now arrives on its own, cheaper, and in the order it is used in
  Changes:
    - Electrostatic adsorption now runs on the Air Filter instead of the Air Compressor. Existing compressors set to that recipe will come back with no recipe selected and their ingredients returned - set them to something else, or replace them with a filter and carry on
    - Wood is now grown rather than conjured. The makeshift extraction recipe that produced a log every 15 seconds from no ingredients at all is switched off once the Arboretum is researched
  Graphics:
    - The Geothermal Turbine has its own model at last. It wore the vanilla steam turbine's picture and icon, which said the one thing about this machine that is not true - there is no steam anywhere in it. It is now a lava-fired engine: a lagged barrel with a glowing feed running up one flank, scorched ochre at the hot end and clean steel at the generator end, three rams working in sequence up the other flank, a spoked flywheel standing clear above the casing and an extractor turning flat on the generator can
    - The XP Lab is no longer a violet copy of the vanilla lab. It is cut stone with amethyst growing out of it: crystal-tipped buttresses at the corners, a reading desk at the front, and a six-sided crystal floating and turning over the central pedestal with four smaller ones orbiting it. The only building in the mod that is not industrial, and the amethyst is the same stone the Crystallizer grows
    - The Air Filter is built to the shape the mod had been carrying a drawing of all along: an eight-sided furnace tower, widest at the ground, with tall grey pilasters standing on its corners, louvred rust panels recessed between them, arched pipes over the throat, and a cleft down the front face that opens onto a lit interior with a small impeller turning at the foot of it. A second impeller turns in a dark hood at the very top. Rust red against the Air Compressor's cold grey, so the two can be told apart at a glance where they stand side by side
    - The Air Filter's plates hang from their caps rather than being bolted on, and the draught through the machine rocks them - each one swinging a little out of step with the next, so the movement travels round the tower instead of every plate flapping at once
    - The Air Compressor shows its own icon everywhere now. It has had a rendered icon since it got its model, but only the inventory used it: on the map, in alt-mode and in Factoriopedia it was still drawing a vanilla assembling machine
    - The Gas Combiner is brass and deep blue against the Air Compressor's cold grey, because the two stand next to each other in any gas build. Two upright receivers of different heights at the back, a mixing drum lying across the front with a lit cyan sight glass in it, a blower wheel standing up on the right and a metering plunger working up and down between them
    - The Crystallizer now shows both of its feeds. The melt arrives in a heavy pipe on one flank and the gas in a thin brass one on the other, so which line goes where can be read off the machine. Its product chute moved to the front, where it can actually be seen
    - The Crystallizer is an open hexagonal hearth: a glowing pan of melt with broken crust floating on it, three rabble arms ploughing slowly through, and six clusters of amethyst crystal standing on the shelf round the rim. The clusters grow and are taken away on staggered schedules, so the ring always has something at every stage. Nothing is drawn over the pan, and the crystals are the only violet in the mod, so the machine can be picked out at a glance among the orange and grey ones
    - The technologies that hand over this release's new machines now show the machine itself instead of a borrowed base-game picture. Stone Crushing, Powered Rock Crushing, Lubricated Rock Crushing, Water Condensing, the Arboretum and the Bio Garden each carry a rendering of what they unlock, shot the way base-game technology icons are - a lower three-quarter view with a soft shadow under it, rather than the flat overhead angle the machine has on the map. The three crushers are told apart here too, because each technology shows its own tier
    - The Lava Centrifuge now has its own animated model instead of reusing the vanilla centrifuge: a squat armoured vessel on a bolted plinth, with cooling fins, a perforated basket spinning behind the drum cage, and three counterweight arms turning on top. The basket is drilled with a staggered grid of holes and the molten charge glows through every one of them, so you can see it turning rather than just guessing. It casts its own shadow, spins while it is working and stands still when it is idle, turns to face the way you rotate it, and throws an orange glow onto the ground around it while it runs
    - The Lava Centrifuge and the Industrialised Chemical Plant now smoke while they work. The plume rises from the tallest point of each machine - the centrifuge's spindle, the chemical plant's tallest column - and thins out as it goes. It only shows while the machine is actually crafting, and it does not speed up when the machine is beaconed
    - The Lava Centrifuge now has a matching icon, so it can finally be told apart from the vanilla centrifuge in the inventory, the crafting menu and Factoriopedia
    - The Air Compressor now has its own animated model in place of the flat, shadowless placeholder image it shipped with: a pressure receiver on saddles along the back, an eight-blade intake fan spinning behind a guard ring, and a finned motor driving it. Deliberately cold grey next to the Lava Centrifuge's orange, so the air route and the lava route can be told apart at a glance on a crowded base. It casts a shadow and turns to face the way you rotate it
    - The Water Condenser has an animated model of its own: two disc-finned cooling columns standing across the back, a six-blade fan pulling air through them behind a square shroud, and a condensate tank with a lit sight glass at the front. Four things move rather than one: the big intake fan, a small extractor on top of each cooling column turning the opposite way, and a condensate pump between them whose ram rides up and down. Light steel and teal, so it can be told apart at a glance from the Air Compressor's cold grey and the Lava Centrifuge's orange. It casts its own shadow and turns to face the way you rotate it
    - The Arboretum is a glasshouse rather than a machine: a walled planter box with a glazed clerestory, an open roof so the four beds of young conifers are visible from above, pink grow lamps under the eaves, and a three-armed irrigation boom sweeping over the beds. The only building in the mod you can see inside. It casts its own shadow and turns to face the way you rotate it
    - The Industrialised Chemical Plant has a proper model at last. What it had before was four entirely different buildings, one per facing, drawn at a camera angle and in a palette of their own - so rotating the machine rebuilt it into something else, and it never looked like it belonged next to the rest of the mod's machines. It is now one building seen from four sides, and a properly crowded one: five hooped vessels of different heights, a spherical accumulator on legs, a reactor drum lying across the front, a three-tier pipe rack threading between them, round galleries with handrails, ladders, valve handwheels and a big elbow dropping to the deck. The drum's drive fan and a smaller extractor on the tallest tower turn against each other while it works, and a feed ram rides up and down
    - The Industrialised Chemical Plant's sight glasses are tinted by the recipe, the way the vanilla chemical plant's are, so you can see what a plant is making from across the base. The windows are spread around the vessels and one sits in the top of the drum, so the colour stays visible whichever way the machine is turned
    - The Algae Tank is four tall culture columns behind glass with nothing standing over them, a circulation pump at one corner and a dosing ram at the other. The columns visibly fill as the algae grows and drain when it is harvested, each on its own schedule, so there is always one nearly full and one nearly empty. The culture is tinted by the recipe, so a tank growing green algae looks green from across the base and a red one looks red
    - The roll crushers are two toothed rolls standing side by side and turning towards each other, with the rock going down the gap between them and the crushed product coming out on an apron at the front. Two rolls rather than one drum, because one drum turning could be a mixer or a kiln, while two turning into each other say in a single frame that whatever goes between them comes out smaller. Nothing at all is drawn over the rolls, so the pair and the gap between them stay visible from every angle
    - Each roll is driven by its own little engine: an eccentric on the end of the shaft, a connecting rod with a round big end wrapped around it, and a crosshead sliding in a cylinder that leans out into the corner. Because the two rolls turn opposite ways the two rods swing in mirror image, which is what makes it obvious at a glance that the rolls are working against each other rather than just spinning. The rods do not move on a simple back-and-forth either - they lean as they go, so the crosshead dwells longer at the bottom of its stroke than at the top, the way an engine actually runs
    - The three crushers are painted apart as well as built apart. The burner's casing is dark rusted iron, the electric one keeps the mod's steel grey and the industrial one is ochre, so you can see which tier a machine is from across the base without zooming in. The three differ in brightness as well as in colour, so they stay apart at night and for a colourblind player. Each also carries its own tell on the drive bed: a firebox with a lit door and a chimney, which is the tallest thing on any of them; a finned motor; an oil tank and a lubricant port. The rolls and their teeth are the same steel on all three, because that is the part that says what the machine does, and three tiers with different rolls would read as three unrelated machines. None of this survives at the size of an inventory slot, so each icon carries a badge in the corner as well - a flame, a lightning bolt, a drop of lubricant - and the three can be told apart in the inventory, the crafting menu and Factoriopedia too
    - The Bio Garden is a ribbed glass dome over a thickener pan, with a rake bridge turning slowly over the pulp inside, a guided press working up and down on one near corner and a discharge hopper on the other. The pulp in the pan is tinted by the recipe and read straight down through the glass, so the colour of what is being pressed shows whichever way the machine is turned, and it churns under the bridge as it goes round. Round and small against the Arboretum's big square glasshouse, so the two read as the same family of building without being the same shape
    - The Air Cooler has been rebuilt. It was the oldest custom model in the mod, drawn without a shadow and at a camera angle of its own, and it had come to look flat beside everything built since. It is now a louvred plant box with two big axial fans turning against each other on the roof, a rime-covered expansion drum lying across the front and a compressor beside it whose piston works up and down under a frosted cap. The frost is the point: this is the only machine in the mod wearing any, which is what marks where the liquid nitrogen line starts. It casts its own shadow and turns to face the way you rotate it

Version: 0.1.7
Date: 2026-09-12
  Features:
    - Added the Industrialised Chemical Plant technology, which finally unlocks the machine of the same name
  Changes:
    - Sulfur on the Lava Block world now uses a dedicated surface property instead of one cloned recipe per known planet, so the vanilla sulfur recipe keeps working everywhere else, including on planets added by other mods
    - Removed 34 leftover Pyroclast prototype files that were split out into the standalone Pyroclast mod back in 0.1.1 and have been unreachable dead code ever since
    - Pyroclast is now an optional dependency instead of a required one. LavaBlock no longer ships any Pyroclast content, and the integration in data-updates.lua was already guarded, so there is no reason to force every player to install it. Load order is preserved, so the integration still applies when Pyroclast is present
  Bugfixes:
    - The Industrialised Chemical Plant was impossible to obtain and would have been useless anyway: no technology unlocked its recipe, the recipe used an invalid crafting_category key, and the entity was restricted to a recipe category that no recipe in the mod uses
    - Fixed the sulfur recipe migration, which used a name-to-name object instead of the list of name pairs the migration format requires; Factorio rejected the whole file, so machines set to the old per-planet sulfur recipes were not migrated
  Balancing:
    - The starting kit now contains 6 solar panels, 3 accumulators and 4 small electric poles. Two panels produced 120 kW while the starting chemical plant and offshore pump together draw 270 kW, so the first factory never ran at full speed and stopped completely every night
    - Air extraction now consumes steam instead of creating compressed air out of nothing, takes 4 seconds instead of 2, and the air-to-ore recipes yield 5 ore instead of 10. The air route no longer makes the entire lava economy redundant
    - Air compressors now draw 1.5 MW explicitly instead of inheriting the assembling machine 3 value, so pulling ore out of thin air trades power for lava as intended
    - Cryogenic lava cooling now produces 1500 steam at 500 C instead of 2000 at 1000 C. The old output contained several times more energy than the lava put into it, which also made the Geo Thermal Turbine pointless. Stone brick byproduct chance reduced from 80% to 50%
